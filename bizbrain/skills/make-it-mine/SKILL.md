# Make it mine

The owner has a BizBrain Template — a general second brain that knows nothing yet. They want it
built around one subject. This turns it into that.

**You do not know what the subject is. Never assume.** It could be stock trading, property,
coaching, farming, panel beating, a church, a charity, a hobby. Everything below says "the
subject". Ask, listen, and use their word for it from then on.

This runs in five stages. **Stage four is the only one that spends real time and money, and you
do not start it until they have said yes to a written plan.** Never run stages one to five in
one breath.

---

## Before you start — check this is the right moment

Read `About me.md` and look at the top level of the folder.

- **If a file called `What this brain is for.md` already exists**, this brain has already been
  made about something. Say so, say what it is about, and ask whether they want to change it or
  add to it. Do not start again over the top of it.
- **If `About me.md` still says `NOTHING SAVED YET`**, that is fine and normal. It fills up as
  you go.

Then say, in your own plain words, what is about to happen: you will ask them some questions,
then show them a written plan, and nothing gets built until they say yes. Then start.

---

## Stage one — what is it for

Talk. Do not fire off a list. **Two questions at a time at most**, following whatever they say.
This is a conversation, not a form. It should take a few minutes, not thirty.

What you need to come out with:

1. **The subject, in their words.** "Stock trading." "Buying houses." "My smash repair shop."
2. **What they want it to do for them day to day.** The actual job. Not "help me trade" —
   closer to "keep a record of every trade and why I took it, and tell me when I'm repeating a
   mistake."
3. **How they already do it.** Their own method, rules, habits, the way they were taught. This
   is the most valuable thing in the whole conversation and the easiest to lose.
4. **What good looks like.** How they will know this brain is earning its place.
5. **What it must never do.** Ask this plainly. Some people want opinions, some want a record
   and nothing else.
6. **Who else touches it.** Just them, or a partner, staff, a bookkeeper.
7. **What they want it called.** Their own name for it. If they have not got one, offer two or
   three and let them pick. Never pick for them.

**Check the spelling of the name once**, out loud, before you write it anywhere.

---

## Stage two — what have they already got

Their own material beats anything on the internet, every time. Ask for it before you go looking
anywhere else.

Ask what they already have: notes, spreadsheets, course material they have paid for, a book
they follow, saved videos, a folder of documents, screenshots, their own written rules, past
records.

**Then tell them exactly how to hand it over, with every step:**

> Open the folder this brain lives in. You will see a folder called **Inbox**. Drag anything
> you want me to have into it — files, screenshots, a document, a whole folder, it does not
> matter what state it is in. Then come back here and say "it's in". If it is a web address,
> just paste it to me here.

Wait while they do it. They may want to do it later — that is fine, say so, and carry on
without it.

Also ask: **is there anything you want me to look up?** A particular writer, a method, a
website, a set of rules they keep meaning to read properly.

---

## Stage three — the plan, in writing, then stop

Show them a plan before spending anything. Plain sentences, short. It covers:

- **The name**, as they chose it.
- **What this brain will be about**, in one paragraph, in their words.
- **The folders.** Which of the seven stay as they are, and what gets added for this subject.
  Say what each new folder is for in one line. Keep it small — a folder nobody uses is clutter.
- **What goes in the Knowledge folder.** List the actual subjects you will write up, by name,
  as a numbered list. Not "research trading" — "1. What a stop loss is and the three common
  ways of setting one. 2. The tax treatment of share trading in Australia."
- **What they gave you**, and what you will do with it.
- **What you will not do**, and why. Say the honest gaps out loud.

**Then say what it costs before you spend it.** Offer three sizes and let them choose:

- **Quick** — the basics only, roughly ten to fifteen written pages, about twenty minutes.
- **Proper** — the recommended one. Every subject on the list done properly with sources, about
  an hour.
- **Deep** — everything, checked against several sources, several hours.

**Then stop and wait for a plain yes.** Do not start building because the plan looks agreed.
If they change something, change the plan and show it again.

---

## Stage four — build it

Only after a yes. Work in this order, and tell them what you are up to as you go.

### 4.1 The folders

Add the new folders from the plan. Leave `Inbox`, `Today`, `People`, `Projects`, `Knowledge`,
`Decisions` and `Done` exactly where they are — they carry the checks and the routines.

### 4.2 What this brain is for

Write `What this brain is for.md` at the top level. It says what the brain is about, what it is
called, what it does day to day, what it must never do, and the date. This is the file every
future session reads to know what kind of brain this is. Add it to `Catalogue.md`.

### 4.3 The rule book

**Add to `CLAUDE.md`. Never rewrite it and never take anything out of it** — a check will
refuse the write and you will lose the lot.

Add one new section at the bottom, headed with the brain's name. It holds only what is true for
this subject and nothing else:

- The words this subject uses, and the words to avoid.
- What goes in the new folders, in one line each.
- Anything the owner said it must never do, in their words.
- Where their own material lives, and that it outranks anything found on the internet.

Keep it under twenty lines. The rule book is already near its useful limit, and past that the
rules get followed less, not more.

### 4.4 If the subject is money, health, law, or anything regulated

Write this into the new section, plainly, and say it to them out loud once:

> This brain keeps the record and does the reading. It is not a licensed adviser and does not
> tell you what to buy, sell, take or sign. Every number it writes down carries the date and
> where it came from, so you can check it.

Never quietly drop this because the owner sounds experienced.

### 4.5 Their own material first

Read everything in `Inbox/` before you look anything up. Write each piece into `Knowledge/` in
their own words, saying where it came from and the date. Then clear the Inbox and tell them
what you filed and where.

### 4.6 Then the research

Work down the numbered list from the plan, one file per subject. Every file follows the rule
book's ten rules, and on top of that:

- **Say where it came from.** Name the source and the date you looked. A fact with no source
  is a fact nobody can check later.
- **Say how sure you are.** Everyone agrees, one source only, or people disagree — and when
  they disagree, write down both sides rather than picking one.
- **Date anything that will go stale.** Prices, rules, rates, tax, anything a government or a
  company controls. Say where the live number lives so a later session goes and looks.
- **Never pad.** A short honest page beats a long one with filler in it.
- **Their way wins.** Where the research contradicts their own method, write both down and say
  so. Never quietly correct them in their own brain.

Add every new file to `Catalogue.md` as you go, not at the end.

### 4.7 Write down the decision

Put a dated page in `Decisions/` saying what this brain was made about, why, and what they
chose. One page.

---

## Stage five — show them, then stop

Tell them what now exists: the name, the folders, how many pages are in `Knowledge/` and what
they cover, and what came from their own material against what was looked up.

**Then say plainly what it still does not know** — the gaps you left, the things nobody could
answer, the numbers that will go out of date and when.

Ask them to open one or two of the pages and read them. **Nothing here is finished until they
have read some of it and said it is right.** Never call it done before that.

Then write today's page in `Today/` so the next session picks up the thread.

---

## Rules that hold for the whole thing

- **Plain English throughout.** They may never have used a tool like this. No jargon, no
  shorthand, no folder names dressed up as concepts.
- **Never hurry them, and never do the whole thing at once to look impressive.** A brain built
  around the wrong subject is worse than an empty one.
- **Two questions at a time, maximum.**
- **Never use their name until they have given it to you, and check the spelling once.**
- **If they stop halfway, write down where you got to** in `Today/` and say it can carry on any
  time. It must be safe to walk away mid-way.
- **Only ever add.** Nothing in this brain is deleted. Replaced files move to `Done/` with a
  note at the top saying what replaced them.

---

## One worked example, so the shape is clear

A person says they want a brain for stock trading and calls it TraderBrain.

The subject is trading. The day-to-day job they want is a record of every trade and the reason
behind it. Their own method is a course they paid for, sitting in a folder on their desktop.
They want it to never tell them what to buy.

New folders: `Trades` — one page per trade, what they bought, why, and what happened.
`Watchlist` — what they are keeping an eye on and why. `Reviews` — what a month or a quarter
actually did.

Knowledge pages: their course, written up in their words, first. Then the terms they use. Then
how share trading is taxed in Australia, dated, with the tax office page named. Then the common
mistakes, with sources.

The rule book gains a short section: the words they use, what the three new folders hold, the
line that this brain never tells them what to buy, and that their course outranks anything
found online.

**That is one example, not a template. The next person will want something completely
different, and the questions are the same.**
