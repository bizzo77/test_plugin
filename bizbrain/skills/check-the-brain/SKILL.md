# Check the brain

The point of this is trust. A brain quietly full of things that were true a year ago is worse
than no brain, because it reads as truth.

**You propose. You never change anything on your own.** Show the list, and change only what
they say to change.

## What to check

**1. Facts that have gone off.** Any claim in the present tense with no date on it — "12
staff", "the busy season", "she's the one who handles that". Each one is either still true or
quietly wrong, and nothing on the page says which. List them and ask.

**2. Files that argue with each other.** The same fact written two ways in two places. Show
both, with their dates, and ask which is right.

**3. The same subject in two files.** One subject, one file. Say which two, and which should
absorb the other.

**4. Projects that have gone quiet.** Nothing written against them for a month or more. Still
live, or finished and never moved to `Done/`?

**5. Files that do not say what they are.** No opening line, no date. These are the ones a
later session cannot judge.

**6. Files missing from `Catalogue.md`.** They exist but nothing points at them, so they are
found only by luck.

**7. Blanks still open in `About me.md`.** Say which, and pick the one most worth asking about
next. Do not ask them all.

## How to report it

Shortest useful version. Group by the seven above, worst first. For each thing, say what is
wrong and what you would do about it.

Then stop. Wait for them to say which ones to do.

## Never

- Never delete, merge or archive anything as part of this.
- Never guess a date to fill a gap. "Not known" is the right answer.
- Never count a blank as answered because you can infer it from somewhere else. Ask.
