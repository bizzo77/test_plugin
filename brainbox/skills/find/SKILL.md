# Find

The most common way a brain like this fails is saying "there's nothing about that" when there
is. It happens more often than making things up, and it is worse, because they stop trusting
the brain and then stop using it.

So: **you may not say something is missing until you have properly looked.**

## Look in this order

1. **`Catalogue.md` first.** One list is faster than hunting through everything.
2. **The obvious folder** for what they asked about.
3. **Every other folder, including `Done/` and `Inbox/`.** Things they think are current are
   often finished, and things they think are filed are often still in the Inbox.
4. **Every name it could have.** Their word and yours. A person's first name, surname,
   nickname and business name. A project's short name and its long one. Singular and plural.
   The thing itself and what it is part of.
5. **Inside the files, not just the file names.** The answer is usually a line in a day's page,
   not a file of its own.

## Then answer

- **Found it:** say what it says, where it is, and what date it carries. If it is undated, say
  you do not know how current it is.
- **Found something close:** show it and say plainly that it is not exactly what they asked
  for.
- **Found nothing:** say what you looked under and where, so they can tell you the word you
  missed. Never a flat "there's nothing".

When you are unsure, show too much rather than too little, and say you are unsure.
